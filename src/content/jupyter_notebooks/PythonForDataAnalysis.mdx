## Why `pandas`?

{<p><big>My main takeaway</big> from <i>Python for Data Analysis</i> is that <code>pandas</code> is flipping awesome, and one of the very few frameworks I've encountered for which I have no reservations about saying the extra abstractions are well worth it.</p>}

I'm not going to put a lot of effort here to justify that enthusiasm, as its evangelism is widespread.
I will just say I now understand why so many libraries have put so much effort into integrating with it deeply, and I would recommend getting to know it well if you are serious about getting fluent with data science in Python.
I myself look forward to getting more familiar with it.

I've used `pandas` before at work, but only in the most basic way, as a convenient wrapper for loading and processing data (usually with `scikit-learn`).

The `DataFrame` is its main table-like data structure.
It is designed to be a direct analogue to [R's data frames](http://www.r-tutor.com/r-introduction/data-frame).
In both frameworks, `DataFrame`s are designed to strike a balance between the expressiveness of SQL queries (hierarchical indices, `select`, `join`, `merge`, etc.), the numerical convenience of matrices and vectors, and the natural table/cell intuition of spreadsheets. `pandas` adds to this set of features a natural integration with Python's built-in language features.

I am more familiar with `numpy`, so I had basically thought `pandas` was a tool to get data from some other format (CSV, JSON, etc.) into a kind of labelled-`numpy` format as quickly as possible.
Without taking the time to understand the philosophy and parlance of `pandas`' core data structures, I _usually_ found the incantations required to access and manipulate them as slightly clunky (except when their awesomeness was immediately obvious, such as their ability to handle missing data seamlessly).
I relied on the DataFrame's `as_matrix()` method to get back to what I considered a more sane representation that can be indexed with a natural `data[i][j]` kind of notation.

However, after getting more familiar with the `DataFrame`, the more fundamental `Index` type underneath it, and the excellent `Series`, which simplifies everything time-related (including well-labelled plotting, time-zone handling and even [resampling!](https://pandas.pydata.org/pandas-docs/stable/generated/pandas.Series.resample.html#pandas.Series.resample)), I am much more comfortable working within these data structures as first-order abstractions without wanting to always pull out the underlying primitive data types and manipulate them manually.
(I still need a _ton_ of practice to really feel comfortable, though!)
